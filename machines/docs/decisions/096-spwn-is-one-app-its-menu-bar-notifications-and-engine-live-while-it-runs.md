# ADR-096: Spwn is one app: its menu bar, its notifications and its engine live while it runs

**Status:** Proposed
**Date:** 2026-11-25

Amends [ADR-076](076-spwn-on-a-machine-one-active-app-one-background-job-the-engine-updates.md), [ADR-073](073-spwn-app-carries-the-engine-on-a-mac-with-a-screen.md), [ADR-064](064-a-key-never-leaves-its-device.md), [ADR-085](085-a-mac-run-alone-keeps-a-device-key-that-signs-with-nobody-present.md), [ADR-090](090-a-machine-is-reached-through-its-engines-door-never-through-openssh.md) and [ADR-054](054-a-machines-standing-is-one-rule-at-each-level.md); supersedes [ADR-058](058-the-window-installs-and-starts-the-core-itself.md) on a Mac. ## Context

A Mac running Spwn shows three programs where the owner wants one:

- **The engine is a background job.** `com.jterrazz.spwn.engine`, `RunAtLoad`, `KeepAlive` and `AbandonProcessGroup` (`apps/desktop/src-tauri/agents/com.jterrazz.spwn.engine.plist`), runs from login whatever the owner does, and stops only on `engine stop` ([ADR-076](076-spwn-on-a-machine-one-active-app-one-background-job-the-engine-updates.md) §4).
- **The menu bar is a second app.** `Contents/Library/LoginItems/Station.app`, bundle id `com.jterrazz.station.menubar`, named "Spwn Menu" (`apps/menubar/resources/Info.plist:9-16`), a nested login item the engine restarts after each update (`apps/engine/wire/engine.go:565`). It posts every notification, under the title "Spwn Menu" (`apps/menubar/sources/station-menu-bar/Model.swift:365`), so Notifications settings lists "Spwn Menu".
- **The window is a third process**, `spwn-window`, which ends with its last window: Tauri ends the run loop when the window map empties unless the app prevents it (tauri-runtime-wry 2.12.5, `src/lib.rs` on `Destroyed`), and `apps/desktop/src-tauri/src/main.rs:18-26` never does.

Docker Desktop and OrbStack, the owner's references, run while they are open, open at login, keep their menu bar item when the window closes, and stop their machines when quit (OrbStack keeps them only under its "Keep running when app is quit" setting).

### What was measured

Lot O0, 2026-11-25, a throwaway macOS 26.6.2 VM on the new Mac mini, made and deleted by `station machines`: an ad hoc signed app whose main executable is C, linking a Swift dynamic library that owns the `NSApplication`, a status item, a non-activating panel, notifications, `SMAppService` and `applicationShouldTerminate`, with children signed with and without the bundle's identifier. No Developer ID, no macOS 27, and not Tauri's `NSApplication`.

- **Notifications take the app's identity.** usernoted resolved the process to the bundle id and named it by the bundle ("app name: O0 Probe"); Notifications settings held one row. The panel took key and typed input inside the same process.
- **A child is the app's for Local Network.** Both children, the one signed with the bundle's identifier and the one on a default identifier, were resolved to the app ("found bundle id … by PID", by the app's own executable UUID; `launchctl procinfo`: the responsible path is the app's main executable). One prompt, one rule keyed by the bundle id, no path rule. A second build over the first kept the rule with no prompt, its first datagram refused then restored, as ADR-073 measured.
- **`SMAppService.mainApp`** lists the app under Open at Login, named by its bundle file.
- **An agent and a nested login item whose files are gone cannot be unregistered.** The new bundle's `unregister()` of each returned `notFound`, error 22 or no answer; both jobs kept running from the deleted files, and their Background Task Management records and their row under App Background Activity stayed after a `launchctl bootout`. Unregistered while the bundle still carried their files, both left, and the next bundle without them left the app's record alone.
- **macOS does not wait for a windowless app at logout or shutdown.** loginwindow skips a UI element from its quit list ("App is UI Element, do not add it"); the app's `terminateLater` was cut after about a second. An app in the Dock was waited for: 20 s at shutdown, then the Mac shut down; at 45 s at logout, loginwindow cancelled the logout, "Application failed to quit".
- **FileVault turns automatic login off** (Apple, "How to log in automatically to a Mac user account": "manual login is required … FileVault is turned on"). The mini reads `FileVault is On.` and has no automatic login.

## Decision

1. **One app, one identity.** `com.jterrazz.spwn` is the only bundle id of Spwn on a Mac: its window, its menu bar item, its notifications, its login item and its Local Network rule. Activity Monitor shows "Spwn" and at most one helper, "Spwn Engine", which has no bundle of its own.
2. **The menu bar panel is a library in Spwn's process.** StationKit and the panel's host are built as one dynamic library in `Contents/Frameworks`, started by `spwn-window` on its main thread, in the same `NSApplication` as the window. The "Spwn Menu" bundle, its login item, its Info.plist and its notification title go. Closing the window leaves the menu bar item; the process lives until Quit.
3. **The engine is Spwn's child.** Spwn starts `Contents/MacOS/Spwn Engine engine run` when no engine answers its hello, adopts one that answers, respawns its child with the existing backoff and, past five failures, shows "Spwn's engine stopped" with Restart. The engine's LaunchAgent is deleted: on a Mac no background job runs Spwn. An app crash leaves its engine running; the next launch adopts it.
4. **Quit stops everything, after a sheet that names what runs.** Quit (the menu bar's, Cmd-Q, the Dock's, `spwn engine stop`) asks the engine for its plan; when something runs, one sheet names it ("2 machines and 1 agent are running. They stop; their disks and conversations are kept."), a server named apart; then the engine stops every VM, Awake, sharing and this Mac's agents, and the app exits after its answer. ADR-076 §4's servers exception goes: the sheet names the server instead. SIGTERM to the engine keeps "exit and leave the VMs for the next engine" (an update, a respawn).
5. **Automations and agents run while Spwn is open.** A job on the engine's tick fires while Spwn runs; a slot missed while it was quit collapses into one run at the next launch. Spwn opens at login, so "open" is the normal state.
6. **The device key's presence is asked once per Spwn launch.** Every "once per engine life" (ADR-064 §1, ADR-085 §2 and its Consequences, ADR-090's client and PT2) reads "once per Spwn launch"; an engine respawn or update inside a launch asks again, as an engine restart does today. ADR-085's unattended key is unchanged.
7. **The command refuses when Spwn is closed.** A call that needs the engine exits 2 with `Error: Spwn is not open.` and `Hint: spwn engine start`; under `--json` it is `spwn.error/v1`, code `engine.not-running`, the same hint, never a prompt. A read that answers without the engine keeps answering; ADR-054's `engine-stopped` reads "Spwn is not open" on a Mac, and `engine-not-installed` leaves macOS. This is root ADR-021 rule 8 applied: no command starts a long-running process. `spwn engine start` opens Spwn in the background, no window, and waits for its hello; `spwn engine stop` is Quit from a terminal; `spwn engine restart` respawns the engine, VMs adopted.
8. **Spwn opens at login through `SMAppService.mainApp`**, under Open at Login and never under App Background Activity. Launched at login it shows the menu bar item only; "Open the window when Spwn starts" is a setting, off by default. A `requiresApproval` status opens the Login Items pane.
9. **A headless Mac opens Spwn at login, with automatic login declared in its Machinefile.** ADR-076 §2 stands: a session, not a daemon, automatic login set only by the owner's own Machinefile. The login item opens Spwn, which runs its engine.
10. **Linux keeps the bare binary under systemd.** A Linux server runs `spwn`'s engine under a systemd user unit; a Linux guest runs none. The same `engine` verbs drive the unit there.

### Open, awaiting the owner

Each question carries the recommendation this record is written on; the owner's answer replaces it.

- **Q1. Do the machines stop when Spwn quits?** a. Everything stops, disks kept, after a sheet that names the machines. b. As a, plus a setting "Keep machines running when Spwn quits", off by default. c. The VMs keep running without Spwn, with no sharing, no door and no watch until the next launch. **a, recommended, awaiting the owner.**
- **Q2. Do agents work while Spwn is closed?** a. Agents and automations run while Spwn is open; at night Spwn stays open (with Awake on a laptop); a slot missed while it was quit runs once at the next launch. b. A small background service stays for automations alone. **a, recommended, awaiting the owner.**
- **Q3. Does the Mac mini keep an always-on mode?** a. The same app everywhere; the mini keeps Spwn open. b. On a Mac whose Never sleeps is applied, the engine stays a launchd job. c. Q1's setting b, on the mini only. **a, recommended, awaiting the owner.**
- **Q4. What does `spwn` do when Spwn is not open?** a. It refuses at once, "Spwn is not open", with `spwn engine start`. b. It opens Spwn in the background, then goes on. c. It asks "Open Spwn?" in a terminal and refuses in a script. **a, recommended, awaiting the owner.**
- **Q5. Does the Dock icon stay when the window closes?** a. It goes with the window and comes back with it. b. It stays while Spwn runs. **a, recommended, awaiting the owner.**
- **Q6. Is "Open Spwn at login" checked by default?** a. Checked and visible on the first-launch card, one click to uncheck. b. Unchecked, turned on in Settings. **a, recommended, awaiting the owner.**

## Consequences

- ADR-076 §4 becomes "no background job on a Mac: the engine is Spwn's child". Its §3 stands, ending in the app's relaunch (`app.relaunch`) after the engine's atomic swap, its new engine adopting the VMs; the menu bar's restart after an update goes, there is no second app. Its Install reads "the first launch sets up the command and, if asked, the login item"; the headless install "opens Spwn in the background".
- ADR-073's mode A agents and nested login item are withdrawn, and "the engine is started by launchd, never as the window's child" is reversed. Its nested-identifier signing rule stands for the engine run alone; as Spwn's child the engine is the app's for Local Network whatever its identifier (measured, ad hoc). The name `Spwn Engine` stands.
- ADR-058 is superseded on a Mac: the window never installs or starts a core on a press; it runs its own engine.
- **The old jobs are unregistered from a release that still carries their files.** Once the files are gone, `unregister()` cannot reach them and their rows stay until the owner's `sfltool resetbtm`. So the migration release keeps `com.jterrazz.spwn.engine`'s plist and the nested `Station.app`, unregisters both at its first launch, and the next release deletes them (ADR-072).
- **A windowless Spwn gets no time at logout or shutdown.** macOS ends it in about a second, and its engine with the session; only a Spwn in the Dock is waited for, and past about 45 s macOS cancels the logout. Decision 4's sheet and orderly stop therefore hold for a Quit the owner makes, not for a logout or a shutdown. What the engine does when the session ends (stop the VMs within launchd's SIGTERM grace, or leave them to tart and read them stopped at the next launch) is left to the lot that builds the Quit path, by probe.
- A FileVault Mac, the mini included, waits at its unlock screen after a power cut, and no Spwn runs until someone unlocks it; automatic login from a Machinefile is reachable only without FileVault. A planned restart uses the FileVault-aware reboot.
- The door, served by the engine, is up only while Spwn is open; a quit or a crash on the mini leaves Jump as the way in once Remote Login is off.
- Not measured, owed before the lot that relies on each: the panel inside Tauri's own `NSApplication` (the menu bar lot's capture rig); two Developer-ID builds for the child's Local Network grant; macOS 27's Dock for a windowless app; whether an upgraded Mac's "Spwn Menu" row leaves Notifications settings once its bundle is gone.
